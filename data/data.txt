The lore of *Elden Ring* is an expansive, dark cosmic tragedy built upon layers of ancient history, divine manipulation, religious fundamentalism, and familial betrayal. Co-created by Hidetaka Miyazaki and George R.R. Martin, the narrative of the Lands Between is intentionally fragmented, buried within item descriptions, architecture, and cryptically spoken dialogue.

The Primordial World and the Outer Gods

Before the era of the Erdtree, the Lands Between was a realm governed by primordial forces and competing cosmic entities known as Outer Gods. Life originally existed in a chaotic, concentrated form called the Crucible, where traits like horns, tails, feathers, and scales sprouted freely on all creatures. During this ancient era, the land was ruled by Ancient Dragons clad in gravel stone armor, led by Dragonlord Placidusax, who served as Elden Lord under a god that eventually fled or was defeated.

The fundamental shift in the world occurred when an Outer God known as the Greater Will sent a golden star crashing into the Lands Between. Carrying a cosmic organism that would become the living manifestation of order, this entity evolved into the Elden Beast, which in turn became the physical embodiment of the Elden Ring—a collection of divine runes that dictated the absolute laws of reality, physics, life, and death in the realm.

Queen Marika and the Foundation of the Golden Order

To enforce its concept of Order, the Greater Will required a mortal vessel. It chose Marika, a woman of the Numen race from outside the Lands Between, elevating her to divine status as Queen Marika the Eternal. To ensure her fidelity and act as her protector and executioner, the Greater Will bound her to a shadow-beast named Maliketh.

Upon ascending to godhood, Marika established the Golden Order. Her most defining act was the removal of the Rune of Death, also known as Destined Death, from the Elden Ring. She entrusted this lethal rune to Maliketh for safekeeping. By plucking death from the laws of nature, souls could no longer die natural or permanent deaths. Instead, when a person's body perished, their spirit and physical remains were brought to the roots of the massive, glowing Erdtree in a process called Erdtree Burial, where they were absorbed and eventually reborn. This granted Marika and her followers functional immortality and cemented the Erdtree as the central pillar of religious, physical, and spiritual life.

The Conquests and Political Unions

To spread the dominance of the Golden Order across the continent, Marika chose a fierce warrior named Hoarah Loux to be her consort and the first Elden Lord, renaming him Godfrey. Together, Marika and Godfrey launched bloody campaigns across the Lands Between. They wiped out the Fire Giants on the Mountaintops of the Giants to seal away the Flame of Ruin—the only force capable of burning the Erdtree—and subjugated various indigenous peoples, including the Omen, the Misbegotten, and the Crucible Knights, whose primordial traits were now viewed as profane mutations by the strict doctrines of the Golden Order.

While Godfrey waged war in the north and south, a mysterious champion named Radagon led the Golden Order’s armies against the region of Liurnia of the Lakes, home to the Carian Royal Family and the Academy of Raya Lucaria, who drew their power from glintstone sorcery and the celestial Primeval Current. Unable to defeat the Carian Queen Rennala in battle, Radagon instead sought peace through union. Radagon married Rennala, repenting for his campaign, and together they produced three child demigods: Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni.

Meanwhile, Marika and Godfrey had their own children, known as the Golden Lineage: Godwyn the Golden, followed by the twin brothers Morgott and Mohg. However, Morgott and Mohg were born cursed with Crucible horns and growths, making them Omens. In accordance with Golden Order doctrine, they were stripped of their royal rights and chained in the subterranean sewers beneath the royal capital of Leyndell.

The Exiles and the Dual Nature of Godhood

Once all major military threats to the Golden Order were neutralized, a shift occurred within Queen Marika. In a sudden act, she revoked the divine Grace from her husband Godfrey and his loyal army of warriors. Stripped of their golden light, Godfrey and his men became the first "Tarnished." Marika exiled them from the Lands Between entirely, commanding them to live, fight, and die in distant lands outside the realm, with a secret prophecy that they would one day be recalled to fight once more.

With Godfrey banished, Marika summoned Radagon from Liurnia to Leyndell to become her new husband and the second Elden Lord. Radagon abandoned Queen Rennala, leaving her heartbroken and mentally shattered, taking his three children with him to be elevated to full demigod status.

Marika and Radagon subsequently had two children of their own: the twins Miquella and Malenia. Because they were born of a single god, both were born afflicted with severe curses. Miquella was cursed with eternal childhood, unable to ever physically mature, while Malenia was born infested with the Rot of an ancient Outer God, causing her body to decay from within.

The connection between Marika and Radagon stands as one of the realm's greatest secrets: they are two minds and personalities occupying a single divine body. Marika eventually grew disillusioned with the rigid control of the Greater Will and the golden dogma she had created, while Radagon remained a fanatical loyalist to the fundamentalist principles of the Golden Order.

The Night of the Black Knives and the Shattering

Dissatisfaction with the Golden Order reached its peak through Lunar Princess Ranni. Chosen by the Two Fingers—the terrestrial emissaries of the Greater Will—as an Empyrean capable of succeeding Marika as the next god, Ranni refused to be a puppet for a cosmic entity. Desiring to break free from her divine destiny, Ranni orchestrated a plot to destroy her own Empyrean physical body.

Using a stolen fragment of the Rune of Death from Maliketh, Ranni imbrued the daggers of Numen assassins—the Black Knives—with the power of Destined Death. On a single night, the assassins struck. Ranni died in body at the exact same moment her half-brother, Godwyn the Golden, was slain in soul. Because only his soul died while his body lived on, Godwyn became a sprawling, lifeless biological nightmare known as the Prince of Death, buried beneath the capital. His corrupted flesh seeped into the roots of the Erdtree, spreading a plague called Deathroot across the world and spawning "Those Who Live in Death."

Devastated by the death of her favored son Godwyn and exhausted by the absolute control of the Greater Will, Queen Marika snapped. Using her hammer inside the Elden Ring's sacred chamber, she shattered the Elden Ring into pieces. Radagon desperately tried to repair the Ring using the same hammer, but failed. As punishment for her rebellion, the Greater Will imprisoned Marika and Radagon inside the trunk of the Erdtree, suspended in stasis.

The Great War of the Demigods

With the Elden Ring shattered, its fragments—known as Great Runes—were claimed by Marika and Radagon's demigod children. Corrupted by the sudden influx of raw divine power, the demigods waged a catastrophic civil war known as The Shattering for total control of the Lands Between.

General Radahn, holding the power of gravitational magic, held back the movement of the stars to halt the fate of the Carian line and defended Caelid.

Praetor Rykard rejected the Golden Order entirely, traveling to Mt. Gelmir and allowing himself to be eaten by an ancient, giant serpent so he could grow strong enough to devour the gods themselves.

Morgott the Grace-Given remained in Leyndell, defending the locked Erdtree and the capital against his own siblings out of unrequited love for a Golden Order that considered him a monstrosity.

Mohg, the Lord of Blood, embraced the bloody power of an Outer God called the Formless Mother. He kidnapped his half-brother Miquella from his cocoon in an effort to elevate Miquella to godhood and rule alongside him as his consort.

Miquella, having abandoned the Golden Order for failing to cure his sister's rot or his own curse, attempted to grow a new divine tree called the Haligtree to provide sanctuary for the outcast and persecuted species of the realm.

Malenia the Severed marched her armies south to confront Radahn. Their legendary clash at the Battle of Aeonia ended in a devastating stalemate: pushed to her limits, Malenia unleashed the Scarlet Rot dormant inside her, blooming into a divine flower that transformed the entire region of Caelid into a toxic wasteland and drove Radahn completely insane with rot.

None of the demigods could claim ultimate victory. The war ended in a brutal, stagnant stalemate. Realizing that none of the demigods were worthy or capable of repairing Order, the Greater Will abandoned them, turning its back on the Lands Between.

The Return of the Tarnished

With the world suspended in total stagnation—unable to die, heal, or move forward—the long-lost Grace returned to the banished Tarnished warriors across the fog. Resurrected from death, the Tarnished are guided back to the Lands Between by the faint light of Grace.

Their directive from the Two Fingers and the guidance of the Roundtable Hold is simple yet monumental: cross the broken continent, defeat the corrupted demigods, claim their Great Runes, bypass the thorny barrier sealing the Erdtree, and reforge the Elden Ring to claim the title of Elden Lord—shaping the destiny of reality according to whichever cosmic force or ideology they choose to champion.
